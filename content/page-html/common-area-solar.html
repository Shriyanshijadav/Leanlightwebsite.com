Common area solar for housing societies

Common area solar is a rooftop solar system connected to a housing society's common-area electricity meter. It runs lifts, water pumps, corridor and basement lighting and other shared services on the society's own generation, and lowers the common bill that residents pay through maintenance.

What is common area solar?

Common area solar is a grid-connected rooftop plant owned by a housing society and wired to the society's common-area connection, so that shared services use its generation before any power is bought from the DISCOM.

The plant sits on the terrace of one or more towers. Its inverters feed the common-area distribution panel, the same board that supplies lift motors, pump sets, lighting circuits, the STP and the clubhouse. A bidirectional net meter on the common connection records imports and exports. The system does not connect to any individual flat.

How common area solar works

The society's loads are uneven through the day, and that decides how much solar it uses directly.

From terrace to lifts and pumps

During daylight the system feeds the common panel and the society draws correspondingly less from the grid. Pumps filling overhead tanks and lifts in the morning peak use solar directly. At midday, when common load is often light, the surplus is exported through the net meter and credited.

After dark, corridor and basement lighting and night lifts run on grid power, offset by the units exported in the day. In a grid outage the inverters shut down, as every grid-connected system must. Where the society runs a diesel generator, a synchronisation controller lets solar run alongside it and cut fuel use.

Who it suits, and who it does not

A good fit when

  • The society is registered and has taken handover from the builder
  • The common-area bill is a large share of maintenance
  • The terrace has usable shadow-free area once tanks and lift rooms are allowed for
  • The general body is willing to pass a resolution

Look at another option when

  • Residents want their own flat bills reduced: compare group and virtual net metering, where the state allows it
  • The society is a row-house or bungalow community where each owner has a roof: individual home systems serve them better
  • The builder still controls the terrace and connection: obtain handover or a builder NOC first

Shifting pump timers into daylight is the cheapest improvement a society can make. It raises the share of solar used directly and reduces what is exported.

Cost and payback for common area solar

A common-area system costs about ₹45,000 to ₹60,000 per kW installed in 2026. A 40 kW system for a mid-size society costs roughly ₹18 to ₹24 lakh, and the ₹18,000 per kW group housing subsidy takes ₹7.2 lakh off, leaving ₹10.8 to ₹16.8 lakh.

That system generates about 4,800 units a month. At ₹8 a unit it offsets roughly ₹38,400 a month, or ₹4.6 lakh a year, so payback falls between about two and a half and three and a half years. The higher the tariff on your common connection, the faster it pays back. Compare capacities on the solar system sizes page.

Common area solar questions

Can the solar system run the lifts during a power cut?

Not on its own, because grid-connected inverters must shut down when the grid fails. With a synchronisation controller it can run alongside the society's diesel generator, as explained under housing society solar.

Should we change when the pumps run?

Yes, where the water supply allows. Filling overhead tanks between 9 am and 4 pm uses solar directly instead of exporting it, which helps most in states that credit exports at a lower rate.

Can the system charge EV chargers in the parking?

Yes. EV charging for residents counts as a common facility under the group housing subsidy, and daytime charging uses solar directly. Plan charger capacity before sizing the system.

How does the committee see what the system generates?

Office-bearers get dashboard access showing daily generation and exports, and a monthly report. A new committee can be given access when office-bearers change.

Who looks after the system after installation?

We do, under the O&M terms in the contract: cleaning, inspections and warranty claims. See comparable society systems on the projects page.
