How to choose a size

Start from consumption, not from roof area or subsidy. Add up the units on your last twelve bills, divide by twelve, then divide by 120. A home using 360 units a month needs about 3 kW.

Then check three limits. The roof: about 100 sq ft of shadow-free area per kW. The connection: most states cap system size at your sanctioned load. And the subsidy, which stops rising at 3 kW, so a larger system is justified by your bill, not by the grant.

  • Round down, not up, if your state pays little for surplus units
  • Allow for an air conditioner or EV you plan to add
  • Ask for the generation estimate in units, not only kW

System size questions

How many units does a 1 kW solar system generate?

About 4 units a day, or 120 units a month, averaged over the year in most of India. Output is higher in summer and lower in the monsoon.

How many solar panels are in a 3 kW system?

Six panels of 550 W each, giving 3.3 kW. With 400 W panels it takes eight. Panel count depends on wattage, so compare systems by kW.

Is a 5 kW system worth it if the subsidy stops at 3 kW?

Yes, if your consumption needs it. The subsidy is the same ₹78,000, so the subsidised share is smaller, but a 5 kW system still pays back in about four to six years for a home using around 600 units a month.

Can I start small and add panels later?

Yes, if the inverter is sized for the expansion or a second inverter is added. A capacity increase needs a revised net-metering approval. See how an on-grid solar system is put together.

What size do I need to run an air conditioner?

A 1.5-ton inverter air conditioner used eight hours a day consumes roughly 8 to 10 units, which is the daily output of about 2 to 2.5 kW of solar. Sizing for a whole home is on the residential rooftop solar page.
