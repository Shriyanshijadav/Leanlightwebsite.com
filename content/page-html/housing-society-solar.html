Housing society solar for common areas

Housing society rooftop solar is a solar system on your society's terrace that supplies the common-area meter: lifts, water pumps, corridor and basement lighting, the STP and the clubhouse. It cuts the society's own electricity bill, which every resident pays through maintenance.

What housing society solar covers

Housing society solar is a rooftop system owned by the society and connected to its common-area electricity connection, so that its generation offsets the units the society buys for shared services.

Settle one distinction first: which meter it serves. A common-area system feeds the society's own connection, the one that runs lifts, water pumps, corridor and basement lighting, the sewage treatment plant, gate lighting and the clubhouse. It does not reduce any flat's electricity bill, because each flat has its own meter. Supplying flats needs either a separate system for each flat or a state that permits virtual net metering. Many committees discuss solar without knowing which of the two they are buying.

How the savings reach residents

Through the maintenance bill. Individual flat electricity bills do not change.

Where the money shows up

The common-area electricity bill is one of the largest items a society pays, and it is recovered from every flat as part of monthly maintenance. When solar cuts that bill, the committee can lower the maintenance charge, or hold it steady and move the saving into the sinking fund for repairs.

In the example alongside, a 120-flat society spending ₹81,000 a month on common-area energy installs 60 kW. The system offsets about ₹64,800 a month, which is roughly ₹540 off each flat's maintenance share. Fixed charges and the units solar does not cover stay on the bill.

Net metering for societies

A society's system is normally net-metered against one connection: the single-point common-area meter. The DISCOM checks feasibility against the local transformer and sanctions capacity against that connection's load, not against the load of every flat in the building. Exported units are credited against what the common meter imports.

Two further arrangements can widen the benefit. Group net metering credits surplus from one system to other connections held by the same consumer, such as a second common meter in another wing. Virtual net metering credits one system's generation to several consumers, such as individual flats, in shares agreed in advance. National consumer rules provide for both, but availability, capacity limits and how credits are settled vary by state and by DISCOM, so we confirm what yours permits before designing. The options are compared on the group and virtual net metering page.

Subsidy for group housing

Group housing societies and resident welfare associations have their own category under PM Surya Ghar, separate from the rates for individual homes. Central assistance for common facilities, which include EV charging points, is ₹18,000 per kW for systems up to 500 kW, with eligible capacity limited to 3 kW for each house in the society. A 120-flat society can therefore claim on up to 360 kW.

The society applies as the consumer on its common-area connection, through the same national portal, and the subsidy is credited to the society's bank account after commissioning. Decide beforehand whether it reduces the project cost or goes to the sinking fund. Eligibility and the application steps are in the PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide. Sizing and roof assessment

We size from the common-area consumption on the last twelve bills, not from the number of flats. Common load depends on how many lifts run, the hours the pumps run and how much lighting stays on overnight, which varies far more than flat count suggests.

A multi-storey terrace gives up area quickly. Water tanks, lift machine rooms, stair headrooms and telecom equipment take space and throw shadows that move through the day, so usable area is mapped hour by hour before a layout is drawn. The structure is checked for the added load, and we ask what residents use the terrace for: drying lines, festivals, a garden. Elevated structures can keep that space usable underneath.

  • About 100 sq ft of shadow-free terrace per kW
  • Capacity rarely exceeds the common connection's sanctioned load
  • Running pumps in daylight lets solar cover more of the load

Cost and payback

Society-scale systems cost about ₹45,000 to ₹60,000 per kW installed in 2026, before subsidy, because design and approval costs are spread over more capacity than a single home. A 60 kW system therefore costs about ₹27 to ₹36 lakh, and the group housing subsidy of ₹18,000 per kW takes ₹10.8 lakh off that.

Payback depends mostly on the tariff the common connection is billed at. Some DISCOMs bill society common areas at domestic rates and others at a separate, higher rate, which shortens payback. In the 120-flat example, a saving of about ₹7.8 lakh a year against a net cost of ₹16 to ₹25 lakh pays back in roughly two to three and a half years, then lowers each flat's maintenance share by about ₹540 a month. Housing society solar questions

Does every flat owner need to agree?

No. The decision is taken by the general body by the majority your society's bye-laws require, not by unanimous consent. Owners who disagree still share the saving through lower maintenance.

What resolution is required?

A managing committee resolution to obtain proposals, then a general body resolution approving the spend and the use of the terrace, and an authorisation naming who signs. We supply drafts; the steps are covered in the PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide.

Can individual flats get their own system instead?

Only where a flat has rights to its own roof area, as in row houses and bungalow societies. In apartment towers, the alternative is virtual net metering where the state allows it. See group and virtual net metering.

What happens to the saving if a flat is vacant?

Nothing changes. The saving reduces the society's common bill, and maintenance is still charged to every flat, occupied or not. Vacancy affects individual electricity bills, not the common-area meter. The system design is explained under common area solar.

Is a society billed at commercial tariff?

It depends on the state. Some DISCOMs bill common areas of residential societies at domestic rates, others at a separate category or a higher rate. We read your bill to confirm before calculating payback with the solar savings calculator.
