Group and virtual net metering for housing societies

Group net metering and virtual net metering let the output of one solar system be credited to more than one electricity connection. For a housing society, virtual net metering is the route by which a shared rooftop system can reduce individual flats' electricity bills, where the state permits it.

What are group and virtual net metering?

Virtual net metering is a billing arrangement in which the units generated by one solar system are credited to several consumers' electricity accounts in agreed shares, without the system being wired to their meters. Group net metering credits surplus from a system at one premises to other connections held by the same consumer.

Both are accounting mechanisms run by the DISCOM. The solar plant connects physically at one point, usually a dedicated generation meter or the common-area connection. The DISCOM reads its generation and applies credits to each participating bill. National consumer rules provide for both arrangements, but each state regulator decides whether, where and up to what capacity they apply.

How the credits are shared

Energy flows into the grid at one point. Credits flow onto many bills.

One plant, many bills

The terrace plant exports its generation through a generation meter. Each billing cycle the DISCOM reads that meter and divides the units among the participating accounts in the shares on file: equal shares for forty flats would be 2.5 percent each. Every participating flat's bill then shows its share of solar units deducted from the units it consumed.

Under group net metering the logic is the same, but every receiving connection belongs to one consumer: a society with two common-area meters in different wings can credit a surplus from one wing's roof to the other. In a grid outage the plant shuts down like any grid-connected system.

Who it suits, and who it does not

A good fit when

  • Your state and DISCOM offer virtual net metering to residential societies
  • Residents want their own electricity bills reduced, not only maintenance
  • Enough flats will sign participation agreements to justify a meaningful system
  • The terrace can carry a system sized to the participants' combined use

Look at another option when

  • The state does not offer it, or not for societies: common area solar lowers maintenance for every flat instead
  • Only a handful of flats are interested: the administrative effort outweighs the saving
  • The society wants subsidy certainty: the common-area route has a fixed group housing rate

Ask the DISCOM, or let us ask, before the committee spends time on allocation. Where the scheme is not notified, everything else is academic.

Cost and saving under virtual net metering

The plant costs the same as any society system of its size, about ₹45,000 to ₹60,000 per kW in 2026. What changes is where the saving lands. Each flat's credit offsets units on its own bill, often at the highest domestic slab the flat reaches, which can be worth more per unit than offsetting the common meter.

Against that, some states levy charges for the arrangement or settle surplus at a lower rate, and subsidy treatment depends on the structure the state allows To confirm: confirm for your state. Group and virtual net metering questions

Is virtual net metering available in my state?

It depends on whether your state regulator has notified it and for which consumers. We check the current position with your DISCOM before any design work, as part of housing society solar feasibility.

What happens when a participating flat is sold?

The share stays with the flat, and the new owner signs the participation agreement. The DISCOM is informed so the credit moves to the new account.

Can flats take different shares?

Yes, if the agreement allows it. Shares are usually equal or in proportion to what each flat contributed, and changes are filed with the DISCOM.

Do participating flats need new meters?

No. Their existing meters stay. Only the plant needs a generation meter, and the credits appear on their normal bills.

Is there a charge for virtual net metering?

Some states levy one, others do not. Any charge is included in our saving calculation. See finished society systems on the projects page.
