What rooftop solar costs and what it saves

A home rooftop solar system in India costs roughly ₹55,000 to ₹85,000 per kW installed in 2026, before subsidy. Housing society systems cost less per kW because fixed costs spread over more capacity.

The short answer

A 3 kW home system, the most common size, costs about ₹1.65 to ₹2.10 lakh and drops to roughly ₹0.9 to ₹1.3 lakh after the ₹78,000 subsidy. Most homes recover that in 3 to 5 years, then run on low-cost power for the rest of a 25-year panel life. Housing societies typically pay ₹45,000 to ₹60,000 per kW and claim ₹18,000 per kW in subsidy on common-area systems, which shortens payback further.

Three things move the number: your tariff slab, how much shadow-free roof you have, and the equipment specified. Compare sizes on the solar system sizes page, or put your own bill into the solar savings calculator.

Subsidy and PM Surya Ghar

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is the central government subsidy for residential rooftop solar. It pays ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, so the maximum is ₹78,000 for any system of 3 kW or larger. The money is credited to the homeowner's bank account after the system is commissioned.

You qualify if you own the home, hold a valid electricity connection in your name, install a grid-connected system and have not claimed a solar subsidy before. Housing societies receive ₹18,000 per kW for common-area systems up to 500 kW. The full rules are in our PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide.

Questions people ask us first

Short answers. Each one links to the page with the detail.

How much does rooftop solar cost in India?

A home system costs about ₹55,000 to ₹85,000 per kW installed in 2026, so a 3 kW system is roughly ₹1.65 to ₹2.10 lakh before the ₹78,000 subsidy. Housing society systems cost about ₹45,000 to ₹60,000 per kW. Compare capacities on the solar system sizes page.

How long does installation take?

The physical installation of a home system takes one to three days. The full project usually takes four to eight weeks, because DISCOM feasibility approval and net-meter installation set the pace. The residential rooftop solar page gives a timeline for each step.

Do you handle the subsidy for me?

Yes. We register your application on the national portal, coordinate the DISCOM inspection and submit the claim, and the subsidy is credited directly to your bank account. Eligibility and amounts are set out in the PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide.

What maintenance does a solar system need?

Mostly cleaning. Dust reduces output, so panels should be washed every two to four weeks in dry months, and the system should be inspected once or twice a year. Our solar O&M service covers cleaning, monitoring and repairs.

Which states do you cover?

LeanLight Solar takes on projects across India. To confirm: List of states with active DISCOM vendor registration. Send your pincode through the contact page and we will confirm coverage and the nearest team.

What happens during a power cut?

A standard on-grid system switches off during a power cut, to protect line workers repairing the grid. If you need power through outages, a hybrid solar system adds a battery and keeps selected loads running.

Is my roof suitable for solar?

You need roughly 100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW, and a roof that can carry the structure. RCC slabs and metal-sheet roofs both work. For large roofs, solar system design and consulting starts with a structural and shadow study.
