On-grid solar system for homes

An on-grid solar system is a rooftop solar plant connected to the electricity grid, with no battery. It runs your home on solar by day, exports surplus units through a net meter and draws from the grid at night. It is the lowest-cost system and the one the central subsidy is designed around.

What is an on-grid solar system?

An on-grid solar system, also called a grid-tied or grid-connected system, is a set of solar panels wired through a grid-tie inverter to a building's electricity connection, operating in parallel with the grid and without batteries.

The panels produce direct current. The inverter converts it to grid-quality alternating current and synchronises it with the supply. The home uses solar power first. Whatever it does not use flows out through a bidirectional net meter, which records exports and imports separately so the distribution company can bill you on the difference. The system connects to three things: the roof-mounted array, the home's distribution board and the DISCOM's meter.

How an on-grid system works

The whole question with on-grid is where each unit goes at each time of day.

The energy flow

Generation follows the sun. Output rises through the morning, peaks around midday and falls away by evening. Consumption does not follow the same curve, so the grid acts as the balancing account: it absorbs the midday surplus and returns it after dark.

The one thing an on-grid system cannot do is run during a power cut. The inverter is required to shut down when the grid fails, a safety function called anti-islanding that protects the people repairing the line.

Who it suits, and who it does not

A good fit when

  • Your grid supply is reliable, with only short and occasional outages
  • You want the lowest upfront cost and the fastest payback
  • You hold a domestic connection and want the full PM Surya Ghar subsidy
  • Your state offers net metering for your system size

Look at another option when

  • Power cuts are frequent or last hours: a hybrid solar system keeps essential loads running
  • There is no grid connection at the site, or supply is only a few hours a day: you need an off-grid solar system
  • You already have an inverter-battery backup and expect it to charge from solar: it will not without a hybrid inverter

If your only concern is an occasional short outage, on-grid solar alongside the inverter-battery you already own is usually the most economical arrangement.

Cost and payback for an on-grid system

On-grid is the cheapest way to go solar because there is no battery. In 2026 a 3 kW on-grid system costs about ₹1.65 to ₹2.10 lakh installed, and ₹87,000 to ₹1.32 lakh after the ₹78,000 subsidy. A 5 kW system costs ₹2.55 to ₹3.20 lakh before subsidy.

Payback is typically 3 to 5 years, against a hybrid system of the same capacity that costs ₹2.5 to ₹4 lakh and takes considerably longer to recover. The difference is the battery, which adds backup but no generation. If you do not need backup, on-grid gives the best return on every rupee. On-grid solar questions

Does an on-grid solar system work without electricity from the grid?

No. An on-grid inverter shuts down whenever the grid fails and restarts when supply returns. If you need power during outages, compare it with a hybrid solar system.

What happens to the extra units I generate?

They are exported through the net meter and credited against the units you import. How unused credits are settled at the end of the billing year depends on your state's net-metering rules.

Can I add a battery to an on-grid system later?

Yes. A battery can be added later with an AC-coupled battery inverter, or by replacing the inverter with a hybrid model. It is cheaper to decide at the start if backup matters to you.

Is net metering available everywhere in India?

Net metering for homes is available in most states, with limits that vary by system size and DISCOM. We confirm what applies at your address before quoting, as part of residential rooftop solar design.

How long does an on-grid system last?

Panels carry performance warranties of 25 years or more and usually keep working beyond that. The inverter typically needs replacing once, after 10 to 15 years. See a completed system on the projects page.
