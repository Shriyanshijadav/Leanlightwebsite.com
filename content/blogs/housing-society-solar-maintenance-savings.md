When a housing society installs rooftop solar, residents often expect their own electricity bills to fall. They usually do not. The saving goes somewhere else, and committees that explain this early find the general body vote far easier.

## Where the saving goes

A society has two kinds of electricity connection: one meter per flat, and a common-area connection that runs lifts, water pumps, corridor and basement lighting, the sewage treatment plant and the clubhouse.

A standard society solar system connects to the **common-area meter**. It cuts the society's common bill, and because that bill is recovered from every flat through maintenance, the saving reaches residents as **lower maintenance**.

## A worked example

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| Flats | 120 |
| Common-area use | 9,000 units a month |
| Tariff (illustrative) | ₹9 a unit |
| Common energy charges today | ₹81,000 a month |
| Solar system | 60 kW, about 7,200 units a month |
| Charges offset | About ₹64,800 a month |
| Saving per flat | About ₹540 a month |

The committee can pass that ₹540 on as lower maintenance, or keep maintenance level and build the sinking fund.

## The subsidy

Group housing societies have their own category under PM Surya Ghar: **₹18,000 per kW** for common facilities, up to 500 kW, within 3 kW per house. In the example, 60 kW earns ₹10.8 lakh. Details are in the [PM Surya Ghar subsidy guide](/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy/).

## What the committee must approve

The terrace is common property, so the decision belongs to the general body. Expect to need a committee resolution, a general body resolution, an authorisation for the signing office-bearer and, if the builder has not handed over, a builder NOC. The full list, and who prepares each document, is on our [housing society solar](/housing-society-solar/) page.

## If residents want their own bills reduced

That needs [virtual net metering](/housing-society-solar/group-virtual-net-metering/), which credits a shared system's output to individual flats. It is only available where the state and DISCOM permit it, so check before the committee spends time on it.
